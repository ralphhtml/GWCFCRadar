================================================================================
1. WHAT IT IS
================================================================================

A browser weather application. One HTML page, 86,240 lines, no build step, no
framework. It runs from GitHub Pages and talks to a parsing server that does the
heavy decoding and model rendering. Free, no accounts required for the map, no
paid tier, no advertising.

Four separate front ends ship from the same repository:

  index.html              The radar application
  forecasting-portal.html The forecaster desk, for issuing outlooks and alerts
  nwrchive.html           The NOAA Weather Radio archive
  assistant.html          The standalone AI assistant

Plus 22 Python pipelines on the parsing server and a Discord bot in Node.


================================================================================
2. RADAR
================================================================================

Four radar sources, chosen from the Radar bubble in the left menu.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 NORMAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The quick national picture. IEM NEXRAD base reflectivity mosaic, plus an
Environment Canada composite for Canadian coverage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 LEVEL 2 (single site, raw)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Raw NEXRAD Level 2 volume scans decoded in the browser, at full super
resolution. A single sweep is about 1.3 million quads and 45 MB in memory.

Ten products:

  Reflectivity              Velocity
  Hydrometeor Classification    Storm Total Accumulation
  1-Hour Accumulation       Correlation Coefficient
  Differential Reflectivity Specific Differential Phase
  Spectrum Width            Differential Phase (raw PHI)

Specific Differential Phase is computed from the raw differential phase
moment in the browser, the way research code does it, rather than read from
a pre-baked product.

Every WSR-88D site plus TDWR terminal radars. The site list is discovered from
the bucket listing at runtime rather than hardcoded, so a new site appears
without a code change.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 LEVEL 3 (single site, parsing server-decoded)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

14 products, decoded on the parsing server and served as georeferenced frames:

  Reflectivity              Velocity
  Correlation Coefficient   Differential Reflectivity
  Specific Differential Phase   Hydrometeor Classification
  Storm Relative Velocity   1-Hour Precipitation
  Storm Total Precipitation Vertically Integrated Liquid
  Echo Tops                 Composite Reflectivity
  Spectrum Width            Hybrid Scan Hydrometeor Class

Tilts 2, 3 and 4 are carried for reflectivity and velocity, and tilt 2 for the
dual-pol products. Terminal (TDWR) products carry their own short and long
range reflectivity and velocity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

are grouped seven ways in the menu:

  WINTER

    Wet Bulb Temp (model), Freezing Level

  SEVERE
    Rotation Tracks at 30m, 60m, 2h, 4h, 6h and 24h
    Low-Level Rotation at 30m, 60m, 2h, 4h and 24h
    Hail Swaths at 30m, 60m, 2h, 4h, 6h and 24h, plus current hail
    Probability of Severe Hail, Severe Hail Index
    AzShear 0-2 km and 3-6 km (live)
    50 dBZ above 0 C and -20 C, 60 dBZ above 0 C and -20 C
    VIL Density, Vertically Integrated Ice, Warm Rain Probability

  REFLECTIVITY
    Composite Reflectivity, Base Reflectivity (QC), Hybrid Scan Reflectivity,
    Hybrid Scan Height, Reflectivity at Lowest Altitude
    Reflectivity at 0 C, -5 C, -10 C, -15 C and -20 C
    Low Level Composite, Layer Composite (low, high, highest)
    3D reflectivity slices at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 km
    3D differential reflectivity at 0.5, 2 and 4 km
    3D correlation coefficient at 0.5, 2 and 4 km
    Echo Tops at 18, 30, 50 and 60 dBZ, High Res Echo Tops
    VIL, High Res VIL, VIL 2h and 24h maxima
    Base and Composite Reflectivity 1h maxima
    Height of Composite and Low-Level Composite

  PRECIP

  LIGHTNING
    Lightning Probability 30 and 60 min
    Lightning Density at 1, 5, 15 and 30 min
    Lightning Jump, Lightning Jump 5m Max

  FLOODING
    CREST Streamflow, Unit Streamflow and Soil Saturation
    SAC Streamflow, Unit Streamflow and Soil Saturation

  COVERAGE

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 RADAR PLAYBACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Up to 3 days of history for radar, satellite and MRMS
  - Frames loaded in a lazy window so a 3-day loop still paints fast
  - Scrubbable animation bar with play, pause, speed, and single-frame step
    forward and back
  - One site-centred bounding box per site and product, so frames do not wobble
    against each other during playback
  - Decoded picture cache, so repainting an already-seen L2 or L3 frame is
    under 50 ms (measured in a test, not asserted)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 CROSS SECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Draw a line on the map and get a vertical slice through the storm, in nine
products: Reflectivity, Velocity, Storm Relative Velocity, Correlation
Coefficient, Differential Reflectivity, Specific Differential Phase, Spectrum
Width, Differential Phase, Hydrometeor Classification.

The slice comes from the full Level 2 volume when the site has one; terminal
(TDWR) radars and products with no Level 2 moment fall back to stacking the
per-tilt Level 3 files. With the Time Machine travelled, the slice is cut
from the archive at that moment. Beam height uses the exact 4/3-earth
inverse, checked against a NOAA archive case.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7 RADAR 3D
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A real volumetric view of a storm, OpenStorm-style: the whole Level 2 volume
is decoded into per-tilt byte planes in the radar's own coordinates, and a
ray-marched voxel renderer draws it as a translucent storm body you can orbit
and cut. Pick a zone with a rectangle on the map, choose any of the volume
products (reflectivity, velocity, spectrum width, CC, ZDR, KDP, PHI), and it
uses the app's own radar colours, including custom palettes. Works live and
from the AWS Level 2 archive, so a past supercell can be flown around. The
marching runs in a worker pool so the map never stalls, and the panel has a
drag handle.

  - Box size is unlimited, with two camera bars (zoom and turn) and a
    Smoothing slider
  - Looks: lit, flat, height, and a Raw pixels look; the strongest cores glow
  - Fullscreen, and Walk mode: step inside the box, with a WebGL-free VR view
    and gyroscope look that work on any phone
  - Shows your own location, and Walk starts you there
  - Plays on the main animation bar like every other layer
  - In a comparison, it follows whichever pane the zone was drawn in
  - A Terrain button puts the real ground under the storm

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.8 COMPARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Compare button on the tool rail opens a menu bar, like the Polygon tool's,
with every comparison in one place.

  SAME LAYER    Radar beside radar (other sites, products or sources), or
                satellite beside satellite (other channels, sectors or
                satellites)
  CROSS LAYER   A different layer in each pane: radar on one side and
                satellite on the other, a model chart beside the radar, the
                waves beside the ocean temperature, and so on

The screen is split into 2, 4 or 8 panes along true split lines. Each line
has a drag grip and a rotate handle that turns only that line (snapping to
45 degrees; a tap turns it 90). Peek hides the dividers for a clean look, and
each pane label dodges the logo, the search bar and the status banner. The
Inspector reads whichever pane is under it, and Radar 3D and Satellite 3D
follow the pane they were drawn in.

3. SATELLITE
================================================================================

34 products.

  ABI SINGLE BANDS (16)
    Blue Visible, Red Visible, Veggie, Cirrus, Snow/Ice, Cloud Top,
    Shortwave IR, Upper Water Vapor, Mid Water Vapor, Low Water Vapor,
    Cloud Top Temp, Fire Temp, Clean IR, IR Longwave, Dirty IR, CO2

  RGB COMPOSITES (14)
    Air Mass, True Colour, Day Cloud Phase, Night Microphysics,
    Dust, Volcanic Ash, Day Land Cloud, Day Snow and Fog, Day Convection,
    Simple Water Vapor, Differential Water Vapor, Day Land Cloud Fire,
    Sulphur Dioxide, Fire Temperature

  GLOBAL (GMGSI, 4)
    Infrared, Visible, Water Vapor, Shortwave IR

RGB composites are built on the parsing server from raw ABI radiances by
satellite_pipeline.py. Satellite has its own preloaded frame pool for
playback rather than mutating a single layer, and ABI loops genuinely play.

Pictures from the parsing server (the Time Machine's archive scans, the
composites and the global mosaic) are plain latitude/longitude grids. Each
one is resampled onto Mercator rows before it is drawn, so clouds sit on the
right coastline instead of about 2 degrees south of it.

SATELLITE 3D
  Long-press the map and draw a zone: the cloud tops rise in relief, built
  from real cloud-top heights (sat_cth.py on the parsing server, or worked
  out in the browser from the Clean IR channel if the server does not
  answer). Any of the 34 products can be draped over the relief. It carries
  Radar 3D's toolkit: fullscreen, Walk and VR, zoom and turn bars, height
  cutoffs, Smoothing, three looks, a cutaway, height units, your location, a
  radar overlay, a Terrain button that raises the real hills under clear
  sky, and a clock button that opens the Time Machine for any past moment.

================================================================================
5. TROPICAL
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 SPAGHETTI MODELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Built from real ATCF a-deck data, not a redraw. Current storms by default with
a past-storms toggle. Name tags connect to their lines with stems so a crowded
basin stays readable. Section-card layout, and a track animation that dots the
forecast out hour by hour with its playbar at the top of the panel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 AI CYCLONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Its own panel and menu entry. Google DeepMind Weather Lab tracks, cross
referenced against NHC names so a track carries the name a person would use.
FNV3 large-ensemble views. GEFS ensemble centres combined into the display.
GEFS, ECMWF ENS and AIFS-ENS all wired in, with ensemble cyclone detection
running on the parsing server.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 TROPICAL OVERLAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NHC 5-day Tropical Outlook with active systems, and JTWC outlooks covering the
western Pacific and Indian Ocean.

6. SOUNDINGS
================================================================================

A full SHARPpy-style panel, opened from the right-click menu anywhere on the
map. Six tabs: Chart, Hodograph, Wind, Numbers, Image, Source.

Backed by sounding_pipeline.py on the parsing server (SounderPy fetch, SHARPpy parameters,
rendered skew-T PNGs per site) with Open-Meteo model soundings as a fallback.

8. OTHER MAP LAYERS
================================================================================

  WAVES     Wave Height, Period, Direction, Swell Height, Sea Surface Temp
  AIR       US AQI, PM2.5, PM10, Ozone
  WIND      Surface Winds, Jet Stream (250mb)
  TEMPERATURE   Air Temp, Feels Like, Dew Point

All five are drawn in the browser from Open-Meteo's live numbers, and every
product's colour scale can be changed in Settings > Open-Meteo Colors (see
section 12).

Sea surface temperature has its own subsystem with four sources (Open-Meteo,
OISST, Coral Reef Watch, AOML), a 1991-2020 climatology, records back to 1982,
anomaly and record variants, and land masking.

NWS SOURCES INSIDE THE BUBBLES
  Each bubble's variables offer their sources: Open-Meteo (live global grid),
  RTMA (the 2.5 km analysis of right now, rendered by the parsing server over CONUS,
  Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico at once), HRRR analysis (the newest hourly
  HRRR runs at their starting hour, 3 km, including an HRRR Reflectivity row
  under Radar), and NDFD (the official human-made forecast). The analyses
  scrub back through recent runs on the animation bar and playback is an
  instant opacity swap, no blink between frames; NDFD plays forward through
  two days.


LAYER 3D
  Every one of these layers has its own 3D panel: model charts, waves, air,
  wind, temperature, pressure, sea temperature and MRMS. Long-press the map,
  pick Draw a 3D (layer) zone, and drag a box: the real ground rises to its
  true height (public Terrarium elevation tiles, with the parsing server's
  cached copy as the backup) and the layer is draped over it, read straight
  off the map in your own colours. The drape keeps following the map, so it
  plays with the animation bar. Ocean layers stand on the real seafloor,
  with a Water button: a see-through sea over it, or the layer painted onto
  it. Each panel has a height stretch (auto, or a slider), lit / flat /
  relief looks, a cutaway, Walk, VR, fullscreen and your location; MRMS has
  its own Time Machine button. Several panels can be open at once.
  The ground heights are tried from four places in turn (two addresses for
  the public Terrarium tiles, Mapterhorn, then the parsing server) and the
  one that answers is remembered; with none reachable the layer still goes
  up on flat ground. A layer picture the page may not read back (MRMS, some
  model charts) is draped from a readable copy fetched in the background.

9. THE AI ASSISTANT AND FORECAST ENGINE
================================================================================

The assistant can see everything on the radar and make a real forecast call
from it, with confidence and reasoning, across nine domains:

  Severe      0 to 2 hours
  Tropical    120 hours
  Winter      48 hours
  Flood       24 hours
  Fire        48 hours
  Marine      72 hours
  Air quality 48 hours
  Comfort     48 hours
  General     168 hours

WHAT IT READS
  - Storm cells found and ranked from the decoded radar mesh (up to 10)
  - Cell motion, tracked across the loop, both from radar and from steering
    flow, and it says when the two disagree
  - Rotation, from both AzShear and velocity couplets
  - Full dual-pol reading per cell
  - MRMS severe products sampled at the cell
  - A sounding pulled for the storm
  - Lightning, satellite, active warnings, sun angle
  - Ocean heat content and genesis potential for tropical work

WHAT IT DOES WITH IT
  - Extrapolates arrival times to the nearest town, with bearing and distance
  - Marks the cells it is talking about, directly on the map
  - Draws a structured forecast card: answer first, evidence underneath
  - Warns you proactively if something is heading for your location, by toast,
    notification and sound
  - Keeps a local record of its own predictions so it can be scored
  - Says exactly what is missing rather than guessing, and answers with the rest
  - Always defers to NWS, and says so once rather than on every line

Also available as a Discord bot (Asturio) and as a standalone page.

10. STORMSTREAM MODE
================================================================================

A broadcast overlay mode for streaming.

  - Coverage-area engine that cycles through active alerts
  - Radar auto-switches to the right site and product for each alert
  - Per-warning-type rules: which layers and overlays come on for a tornado
    warning versus a flash flood warning, editable in a panel
  - HUD with gradients and gold outlines
  - Alerts panel auto-scrolls in step
  - Skip controls, and a privacy warning before it starts

12. SETTINGS
================================================================================

17 tabs, 31 on/off switches:

  Map                     Radar (with Radar Colors)
  Model Colors            Satellite Colors
  Open-Meteo Colors       Layer Opacity
  Particles (wind, wave)  Units
  GPS                     Alerts
  Keyboard                Ocean Colours
  Layer Order             StormStream (with Per-Warning Setup)
  App Colors              AI Chat
  Discord Rich Presence

The colour editors take variable-length gradient stops, invert, presets and a
live preview, and work identically for radar, model fields and the Open-Meteo
layers.

OPEN-METEO COLORS
  Waves, Air, Wind, Temperature and Pressure each get their own colour table
  per product (wave height, swell, period, sea temperature, US AQI, PM2.5,
  PM10, ozone, air temperature, feels like, dew point, wind speed,
  pressure): 13 in all. Each colour starts at a value you can type, colours
  can be added, removed, inverted or taken from a preset, and the layer on
  the map repaints the moment you edit, from the numbers it already has.
  Because these layers are painted from numbers in the browser, the
  recolouring is exact, and the Inspector and Layer 3D follow it. Saved on
  the device, with Back to Normal for one product or all of them. Custom radar
colours, the filter and model colours sync to the account and follow you to
any device; an uploaded .pal file always outranks the cloud copy.

THEMES AND THE LOOK
  The default look is black and gold: black gradient panels, buttons in the
  animation bar's cyan so the pressable controls read as one family, the
  overlay button and its pills in gold, the layer bubbles in their signature
  red, and the account pill black with a red outline. The old red look ships
  as the Ralph's Red preset, beside 30-plus other whole-look presets and a
  full per-token, per-surface, per-part theme editor with import/export.
  Settings also carry a 12-hour or 24-hour clock (the time stamp follows the
  chosen timezone), a custom pointer (tornado, hurricane or supercell), and
  a broadcast sweep beam over the active radar site.
  Liquid glass runs at a subtle frost by default on every theme: floating
  surfaces are translucent, tinted by the active theme's own gradients, with
  a backdrop blur (switched off on touch consoles where blur is the biggest
  GPU cost).

13. THE PARSING SERVER (22 pipelines)
================================================================================

  radar_pipeline.py      NEXRAD L3 and the 163-product MRMS catalogue
  gfs_pipeline.py        66 models, 97 fields, regional nests
  satellite_pipeline.py  RGB composites built from raw ABI
  sat_archive.py         NOAA archive scans for the satellite Time Machine
  sat_cth.py             Cloud-top heights for Satellite 3D
  terrain.py             Cached elevation tiles, the backup for every 3D view
  cities_pipeline.py     Every named town on Earth, tiled for the forecast dots
  sounding_pipeline.py   SounderPy fetch, SHARPpy params, rendered skew-Ts
  sounding_service.py    Live sounding endpoint
  model_sounding.py      Model-derived soundings
  sst_pipeline.py        SST and ocean heat (OISST, Coral Reef Watch, AOML)
  cyclones_pipeline.py   AI cyclone tracks
  enscenters_pipeline.py Ensemble cyclone centre detection
  spaghetti_pipeline.py  ATCF a-deck spaghetti tracks
  feeds_pipeline.py      Outlooks, discussions and text products
  nwr_archiver.py        NOAA Weather Radio recording
  nwr_index.py           NWR archive index
  serve.py               HTTP server and the Discord relay
  scan_sources.py        Source discovery
  check_models.py        Model availability checks
  publish_url.py         Address publishing
  tunnel_doctor.py       Cloudflare tunnel diagnosis

Retention is age-based, not count-based: 3 days of radar, satellite and MRMS
frames. Pipelines run under a time budget and prune when disk passes a
threshold, so a slow pass degrades instead of filling the card.


================================================================================
14. THE DISCORD BOT (ASTURIO)
================================================================================

Runs the same brain as the in-app assistant, through the same Cloudflare
Worker, so no API key lives on the machine.

  /ask <question>     Any weather question, grounded in live NWS alerts and
                      SPC storm reports fetched before answering
  /alerts             Nationwide NWS alert summary
  /map <options>      A map picture, and the options are generated from
                      index.html so the command cannot offer something the
                      site does not have
  @Asturio            Same as /ask, by mention

It also publishes a roster to Firestore so the website's chat can offer real @
mentions. Pings are constrained on the parsing server: allowed_mentions carries an empty
parse, which makes @everyone, @here and role pings impossible whatever the
message says, plus a users allowlist of at most 8 validated ids.

  /economy            A weather-themed economy game
  Embeds              Every AI reply arrives as an embed, with a rotating
                      gold, red and blue sidebar and big markdown headers

DISCORD RICH PRESENCE
  Opt-in, through a small local bridge: your Discord status shows what you
  are looking at, like single-site radar, Radar 3D, or which layers you are
  comparing.

16. ENGINEERING
================================================================================

  - 183 test files in tools/, mixed Node and Python
  - Service worker: network-first app shell with a timeout fallback,
    cache-first for CDN assets and fonts
  - Installable as a PWA
  - Measured performance: first map tile at 397 ms, 489 ms of long tasks
    during boot, LCP 0.23 s and CLS 0.02 on a warm load
  - Every user-visible layer carries an info button explaining what it is, in
    plain English rather than jargon
  - A changelog modal keeps the full history of what changed; it opens itself
    once for a returning visitor when something new has shipped, and is
    always available from the Updates button in the account panel
  - Hidden radar animation frames step off the map during a zoom and return
    in quiet batches after, so zooming with radar on stays smooth
  - Preloaded playback frames that are not on screen skip tile updates while
    a pan is under way and catch up when it ends, and are not composited by
    the browser at all; satellite refills its playback window nearest frame
    first in idle time after a zoom instead of in one burst
  - The animation bar's time labels are measured by a ResizeObserver rather
    than on every frame, and only rewritten when they change
  - The mouse wheel zooms like a pinch: the map glides, and tiles load once
    for the level you stop on instead of for every notch along the way
  - Offline mode: every layer replays its last good copy, a banner says since
    when, and a reconnect catches up by itself
  - A memory guard sizes the frame pools to the machine, and a hidden tab
    sheds its frames
  - A first visit is one popup, not a chain of them
  - No em dashes anywhere in the site, by rule
  - Searching a city flies the map there and opens its forecast
